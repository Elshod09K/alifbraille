The problem

The alphabet changed.
Braille didn’t.

01

Exams moved to Latin

Uzbekistan’s schools, textbooks and university entrance exams now use the Uzbek Latin alphabet.

02

Braille stayed in Cyrillic

Most tactile books in specialized school libraries were produced in Cyrillic Braille.

03

Teachers can’t check

Many teachers of blind students are sighted and don’t read Braille by touch, so they can’t verify a worksheet.

Blind students get fewer practice papers, later, in the wrong script, for the same exam everyone else takes.

Why existing tools fail

One wrong cell changes the answer.

Uzbek wordLetter-by-letter conversionAlifBrailleWhat goes wrong
o‘g‘il“son”
Reads the apostrophes as punctuation. O‘ and G‘ are letters.
shahar“city”
Splits sh into two cells. In Uzbek it is one letter.
2027“year”
No number sign, so it reads as the letters b-j-b-g.

The solution

Three steps.
Any teacher.
Any computer.

  1. Type or paste Uzbek Latin text. Braille appears as you type, in about 4 ms.
  2. Check by ear. The app reads aloud and lights up each cell as it speaks.
  3. Print a calibrated A4 sheet for swell paper in one click.

Runs in the browserWorks offlineFree · MIT license

Built for sighted teachers

You don’t need to read Braille to check it.

As the text is read aloud, each Braille cell lights up in gold. A teacher hears “b” and sees the matching cell, so mistakes are caught on screen, not by a student’s fingertips on exam day.

sh
sh = one cell
Uzbek letters like o‘, g‘, sh and ch each map to a single, correct cell.

Accurate to the millimetre.

2.5 mmdot pitch
10 mmline pitch
A415 mm margins
50 mmruler on every sheet

Sheets follow the Marburg Medium standard for swell paper. Printers often shrink pages without asking; the 50 mm ruler shows it before an expensive imported sheet goes through the heater.
